These are six comparisons on two known development bodies and three seeds. The experiment chooses the first-visit checkpoint; this is not automatic memory retrieval. These trials do not establish how other creatures or unfamiliar worlds will behave.

Look at the evidence behind this case +

All three branches share the same first 120 seconds, including the move from A to B at 60 seconds. At 120 seconds the light returns to A. “Keep learning” continues updating its action values. “Current values” fixes the values reached just before the return. “First-visit values” restores the values saved at 60 seconds and fixes them. Fixed values still guide actions from current sensor feedback; they do not mean a motionless motor or a prerecorded movement.

The experiment selects the earlier checkpoint. It tests the usefulness of those saved values, not a creature’s ability to recognize a place and retrieve a memory by itself. The comparison checks matching recorded physical state, a complete learner-state fingerprint before the branch, and preservation of nonparameter learner state when the earlier values are restored.

Curves retain received-light samples from the native report. Reward and electrical energy come from the report’s integrated measurements. The faint band marks the primary 120–140-second window. This page reads an exported record and does not run or change a creature.
