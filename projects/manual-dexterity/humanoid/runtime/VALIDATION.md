# Native walking evidence — 2026-09-16

The G1 walks in actual MuJoCo contact dynamics under Unitree's published
controller and a locally trained imitation student. The simulator, controller
inference, evaluator, PPO trainer and imitation trainer are C++17. The browser
displays JPEG frames generated by that simulator.

The expert-assisted imitation student started with random weights and learned
from the published controller's action labels; no teacher parameters were copied.
Twenty rounds collected 80,000 labeled steps and made 65,800 CUDA optimizer
updates in 464.54 seconds. Round 19 was selected using seed 10002, then its
exported policy was independently evaluated on seeds 0–4. Four trials passed;
seed 1 fell at 16.66 seconds. This is locally learned walking on these measured
flat-ground trials, with an observed stability failure. It is not unassisted PPO.
See the [independent evaluation](validation/evaluation-imitation-history8.json),
[training record](validation/imitation-history8-run.json), and
[method and reproduction](../training/DISTILLATION.md).

The evaluated and published imitation policy SHA-256 is
`a77e672689271cf91907be2a2d058bb3e3cd2b865629d440041ba531e91ed1bc`.
The training record's `walking_proven: false` was written before independent
evaluation and remains unchanged; the separate evaluation records the 4/5 result.

A diagnostic replay of the canonical initial pose measured a maximum applied
normalized action of 2.3197 for the published controller (0.580 rad position
offset), versus 0.7294 for the 327,680-transition PPO checkpoint (0.182 rad).
The PPO actor is bounded to ±1, but this checkpoint had no applied actions above
0.95 in magnitude. Its observed failure cannot be attributed to hitting that
bound; nevertheless, its action space cannot reproduce the full range used by
the published gait. See the [baseline action measurements](validation/action-range-baseline-cpu.json)
and [PPO measurements](../artifacts/training/native-327680-screen/action-range-cpu.json).

Walking requires the entire 20-second trial without a fall, at least 5 m
forward travel for the 0.5 m/s command, five touchdowns per foot, eight
alternations, and at most 10% airborne samples. A touchdown requires 60 ms
without foot contact; simultaneous landings do not count as alternation.
No trial automatically resets after a fall. The five seeds perturb the initial
leg angles of one checkpoint, rather than representing five trained policies.

## Native runtime and GPU

The running container is `humanoid-mujoco-native`, with the approved browser
port [127.0.0.1:43871](http://127.0.0.1:43871/). Its immutable ID, image ID,
bind mount, labels, restart policy and GPU request are recorded in
[container.json](validation/container.json). The authoritative launcher
recognized the existing managed container without replacing it.

Native LibTorch detected one RTX A2000 Laptop GPU and executed a CUDA dot
product with result 16. MuJoCo physics and OSMesa rendering run on CPU;
policy inference, PPO and imitation neural-network updates run on CUDA. No `python` or
`python3` executable exists in the container. The setup script installs only
reproducible dependencies; the launcher and `run.sh` own project operations.

All native targets compiled with Debian GCC 12. The CTest GAE checks passed,
including true-terminal versus time-limit bootstrapping and episode isolation.
The smoke performed 128 optimizer updates, changed parameters by L2 norm
4.5562, and exported/reloaded a TorchScript policy entirely in C++.

The API rejects invalid controls, out-of-range speed, non-JSON requests and
foreign origins with the expected status codes; see
[HTTP checks](validation/http-checks.json). Browser checks confirmed the live
camera, pause control, checkpoint selection and synchronization of a changed
speed command after reload.

## Viewer performance

The native renderer's revised 640×426 camera preserves the complete body.
It disables expensive display effects and duplicate collision visuals, caches
paused frames, and lets fixed physics steps catch up between rendered frames.
The [render benchmark](validation/render-benchmark.json) measured a 125.36 ms
mean warm frame while the training and old viewer processes were running.
The first frame includes graphics initialization. The page reports measured
simulation time relative to wall time rather than assuming real-time playback.
After deployment, the viewer measured **0.955× real-time simulation and 8.77
frames/second**, with 50.09 ms frame rendering; see
[live measurements](validation/viewer-performance.json). A complete browser
trial reached 30 seconds, 13.69 m and 73 alternations without falling.

On 2026-09-17, the same stopped container was restarted through the authoritative
launcher; its immutable ID was unchanged. Startup checks passed, Docker reported
healthy, and the native CUDA check again returned 16. The browser's imitation
selection loaded the exact evaluated checkpoint and displayed live walking with
the matching evaluation link. See [the replay check](validation/viewer-imitation.json)
and [its camera frame](validation/imitation-live.jpg).

## Relation to the original brief

The later history PPO feasibility attempt stopped on 2026-09-17 after the user
bounded it to two further training invocations. The selected checkpoint survives
20 seconds and advances about 0.85 m with alternating foot lifts, but the replay
shows swivelling and substantial slipping; walking was not convincingly proved.
See [the bounded result](PPO_BOUNDED_RESULT.md). No further training is planned.
The later trainer includes an asymmetric critic and observation history; the
description below refers to the earlier baseline experiment.

Implemented: a free six-degree-of-freedom base, physical foot contacts, twelve
leg motors with faster PD control, policy observations/actions, native PPO and
GAE, supervised imitation with data aggregation, checkpoint export, continuous
evaluation, and a passive comparison.
The baseline uses the supplied recurrent controller; our PPO actor is a small
MLP with the same 47-input interface. Our imitation student uses eight causal
observation frames, with history maintained by its native TorchScript export.
Upper-body joints are fixed in this model.

Transformer history, an asymmetric critic, terrain and dynamics randomization,
push recovery, turns, and hardware transfer remain outside this first measured
experiment. These flat-ground results do not establish those capabilities.
